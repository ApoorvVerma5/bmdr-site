Collect the images off a page. Filtered, renamed, downloaded.

A Chrome extension for people who work with a lot of images: your own site's assets, licensed stock, client photo sets, research material. It gathers the images the page has loaded into your browser, then lets you filter, convert, batch-rename and export them as one organised ZIP instead of right-clicking a hundred times.

Who it is for

  • Designers and developers pulling assets off a site they own or build for.
  • Anyone who has bought or licensed a set of images and needs them in a usable form.
  • Researchers and archivists collecting openly published material.
  • Anyone tidying up a batch of files: consistent names, consistent format, one archive.

What it does

  • Sees the page the way your browser does. It reads the images already loaded and rendered on the page you are viewing, including ones set as CSS backgrounds or placed inside web components, which a plain right-click can miss.
  • Filters precisely. By width, height, aspect ratio, file type and file size, so you skip icons, spacers and tracking pixels.
  • Converts WebP. Turns WebP into PNG or JPG on your own device, so you get files your tools actually open.
  • Batch renames. Prefix, suffix, sequence numbers with zero-padding, and an option to keep original filenames.
  • Exports as ZIP. One organised archive instead of a hundred loose downloads.
  • Stays on your machine. No account, no sign-in, no analytics, no server. Everything runs locally in your browser.
What it is not. The extension only handles images your browser has already loaded on a page you have open. It does not bypass paywalls, logins or any other access control, and it does not give you rights to anything you download. You are responsible for making sure your use of an image is permitted.
